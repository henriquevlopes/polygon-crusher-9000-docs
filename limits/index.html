Polygon Crusher 9000
  • Limits

Limits

Polygon Crusher 9000 does not always reach the target. This page lists what can stop it, and what the report tells you.

After every crush, Blender's status bar shows a report: triangles before and after, time taken, and any warnings. Always check the "after" count.

The result can stop above the target

The crush aims for your target, but some things stop it early:

  • Protect Area. Strong protection on large areas can leave the result above the target.
  • Broken geometry. Duplicate or zero-area faces block collapses. Clean Mesh runs by itself on meshes with many duplicate vertices or zero-area triangles. Otherwise the report says the mesh may be broken and suggests Clean Mesh. If Clean Mesh is already on, it says the mesh cannot be reduced further without breaking its shape.

The report only raises this warning when the result is clearly above the target (more than 20% over), so small differences do not show up as warnings.

Kept edges (hard edges, material borders, UV seams) do not stop a crush above the target: a kept edge is a cost, not a lock. What a very low target costs on such a model is shape, not triangles: see What a very low target costs.

Small pieces at heavy reductions

A model made of many separate pieces (a pole with its insulators, a set of screws) loses whole pieces when the target is a small fraction of its triangles. Since 2.7.1 every connected piece keeps a minimum number of triangles until the error budget is truly spent, and then pieces go in order of how little they matter, smallest first. At an extreme target pieces still disappear; raise the target, or put a Protect Area on the ones that must stay. Small Pieces under Clean Mesh is a different thing: it deletes loose bits before the crush, on purpose.

Objects can be skipped

An object is left untouched, and named in the report, when it:

  • has no geometry after modifiers,
  • has fewer than 4 triangles, or
  • gets no triangles in a Budget crush.

If every selected object is already at or below the target, the copies are left unchanged and the report says so.

Scans and noisy meshes: hard edges

On a scan-like mesh, almost every edge has a small bend because of surface noise. Treating all of them as sharp would lock the whole mesh.

So the Scan, Organic and Cloth sources do not keep angle-sharp edges, and on a model that reads as a scan under another source, angle-based detection is skipped as well. Only the edges you marked Sharp in Blender are kept. Show Hard Edges says "Looks like a scan: only marked Sharp kept".

If you want specific edges kept on a scan, mark them Sharp yourself first.

Sharps

  • Sharps is skipped on sources over 1 million triangles.
  • On a low-poly model where bevel edges cover most of the surface (a game weapon), the bevels ease off so the shape is still followed. For crisp hard edges there, use Decimate rather than Remesh.
  • A hard-surface source falls back to the usual method (not the HiPoly-style one) with Textures on, several materials, Symmetry, protected areas, keeping under 5%, or low-poly kits it cannot bring down to the target. See Source Types.

Very low targets on dense CAD

Very low targets on dense CAD meshes, a few hundred triangles, can fold the walls of holes together. This leaves edges that are shared by three or more faces (non-manifold edges).

The report warns when a crushed copy has more of these edges than its source. If that matters for your export or for baking, raise the target.

Remesh

  • Remesh makes quads (and, on models of many loose parts, some triangles joined into quads). It lands close to the count you ask for, usually within a few percent. A CAD part with many holes can need more quads than you asked for (a block with many holes needs at least about 1,240); the status line says so.
  • A quad remesh rounds off fine detail finer than a quad. On low-poly hard surface with many bevels, Decimate keeps hard edges crisper.
  • Thin walls and bars at low counts can tear. Models that are not one clean surface (triangle soups, self-crossing sheets) can fail or come out as loose pieces. Switch on Rebuild Surface for those: it rebuilds a closed skin, but loses detail finer than its grid and your Sharp marks.
  • Remesh does not use LODs, Clean Mesh, Protect, Import and Crush or the flat-area options.
  • The quad engine's result differs slightly between processor types (Apple Silicon vs Intel). The same computer always gives the same mesh.
  • On Linux ARM64 there is no quad engine, so only Decimate is available.
  • Quad Density: a part smaller than a big quad loses its shape under Less Detail.

Baking

  • Base Color is baked from the image textures feeding each material's Base Color, or from vertex colors when a material has none. Colors made by procedural nodes (Noise, Gradient, a plain color value) are not baked. An object with neither textures nor vertex colors is skipped for Base Color, and the report says so.
  • A bake needs UVs on the crushed mesh. Meshes without UVs are unwrapped automatically. See Bake Maps and Auto UVs.
  • The baked maps are connected in copies of the materials. If an input was already connected there, the old link is replaced.
  • The bake happens after the crush. A failed bake is listed in the report as a note and the crushed mesh is kept.

Rigs, shape keys and vertex colors

Skin weights, the Armature modifier, shape keys and vertex colors carry over to the crushed copy, with a few limits: shape keys are dropped when a modifier changes the vertex count, and vertex colors are skipped on sources over 3 million triangles. See Rigs, Shape Keys and Vertex Colors.

Textures (Keep UVs)

Textures is off by default. Turn it on only when the result must keep its UV layout. UV transfer quality depends on how much the topology changed. Moderate reductions give the cleanest results. UV seams are held in place and collapses that would fold a face's texture mapping are refused. The crush relaxes those rules in stages once it runs out of safe collapses, so the target is reached; the last few percent of a very low target can then show some stretch near seams.

Revert

Revert needs the original. It only works when the crush was made with Keep Original on, or with LODs. See Keep Original and Revert.

Import and Crush

  • Reads .obj, .ply (ascii and binary) and .stl (ascii and binary). Other formats (glTF, FBX, USD) have to go through Blender's own importer.
  • Normals in the file are ignored; the result is shaded smooth. Shading, Protect, Bake and LODs do not apply to an imported file.
  • A texture is only connected when the image is next to the model and Keep UVs (and textures) is on. Textures other than the base color (map_Kd) are not read.
  • Faces with more than four corners are split as fans. A concave n-gon can come out wrong; scans do not have those.
  • Memory: the engine needs about 1 GB of RAM per 3 million triangles in the file. A 32 million triangle OBJ (2.5 GB on disk) peaked at 10 GB and crushed to 200,000 triangles in 72 seconds. On a 16 GB machine, stay under about 40 million triangles.

Platform

  • Blender 4.2 or later. 4.2 is the lowest version the extension accepts and has not been tested; the add-on is tested on Blender 5.2.
  • Windows x64, Linux x64, Linux ARM64, macOS Apple Silicon and macOS Intel have a bundled Decimate engine. Remesh (the quad engine) is bundled for Windows x64, Linux x64 and macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel), not for Linux ARM64.
  • The add-on needs its native binary. There is no Python fallback. If the binary is missing, the panel shows "Native binary not found" and Crush does not run.
