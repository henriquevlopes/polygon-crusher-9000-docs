Clean Mesh

AI-generated and scanned meshes often have broken geometry. When you crush them, they fall apart into fragments or stop far above the target. Clean Mesh repairs the mesh before the crush.

Turn it on with the Clean Mesh checkbox in the Clean Mesh group of Advanced. Scan mode turns it on for you, and Auto turns it on when it finds something to repair.

What it does

  • Merges duplicate vertices.
  • Removes broken faces (zero-area faces) and duplicate faces.
  • Fixes flipped faces.
  • Deletes small floating pieces (floaters).

Remove Small Pieces

The Remove Small Pieces slider (labelled Small Pieces in the panel) sets how small a loose piece has to be to get deleted. It is a share of the mesh's triangles.

  • Default: 0.1%. Range: 0% to 5%.
  • 0% keeps every piece.
  • Greyed out unless Clean Mesh is on.

Raise it when a scan has lots of floating bits. Keep it low for models that really are made of separate parts, like a set of small screws.

Runs on its own when a mesh needs it

You don't always have to switch it on. Clean Mesh runs by itself on an object (and the report says so) when:

  • 2% or more of its vertices are duplicates. Scans from glTF, FBX and OBJ are often split along UV seams; crushing them without welding opens cracks along the seams.
  • 2% or more of its triangles have no area. This is typical of an OBJ saved with too few decimals, or a scan snapped to a coarse grid. Without cleaning, the crush would stop with "Unable to reduce mesh".

Welded meshes (most CAD parts and sculpts) score 0% and are left alone. Turn the checkbox on yourself for other broken meshes, for example flipped faces or floating debris.

Scan Repair

Scans bring their own kind of damage that welding does not touch. Under Clean Mesh there are three switches for it. They need Clean Mesh on, work with a crush from the panel and with Import and Crush, and the report says what they did.

Cut Floor

The table, turntable or ground captured under the model is removed. With Height at 0% the floor is found by itself: it is the dense, flat layer at the very bottom of the model. If the scan has no such layer, nothing is cut and the report says no floor found. Set Height to cut at a share of the model's height instead (everything below that goes).

A ground scan (a patch of terrain) has a dense flat top of its own. That is not mistaken for a floor: only the lowest dense layer counts.

Remove Spikes

Two fixes in one switch:

  • A point shot far out of the surface (farther than twice the size of its neighbour ring) is pulled back to the centre of that ring.
  • A long stretched face, with an edge more than ten times the typical edge of the mesh, is deleted. Scanners make these where two surfaces got bridged. The small hole left behind is closed by Fill Holes.

Fill Holes

Small missing patches are closed with a fan of faces around a new centre point. Size is the longest outline a hole may have to be filled, as a share of the model's size (default 5%). Bigger openings stay open: the bottom of a bust, the back of a relief, the edge of a ground scan. The report counts the holes filled and the openings left open.

Order

Floor, spikes and stretched faces are handled first, then small floating pieces are removed, then holes are filled, then the winding is fixed so the new faces point the right way. The crush runs on the repaired mesh.

Only the copy changes

Clean Mesh only changes the crushed copy. Your original is never repaired or edited.

When something was fixed, the report in the status bar lists it, for example how many vertices were merged and how many bad faces were removed.

When to use it

  • A crush ends far above the target and the report says the mesh may be broken: try Clean Mesh.
  • The crushed result has holes, spikes or fragments that the original does not.
  • The mesh comes from an AI model generator or a photogrammetry scan.

If you already have Clean Mesh on and the report still says the mesh stays above the target, the mesh cannot be reduced further without breaking its shape. See Limits.
