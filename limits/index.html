Polygon Crusher 9000
  • Limits

Limits

Polygon Crusher 9000 does not always reach the target. This page lists what can stop it, and what the report tells you.

After every crush, Blender's status bar shows a report: triangles before and after, time taken, and any warnings. Always check the "after" count.

The result can stop above the target

The crush aims for your target, but some things stop it early:

  • Protect Area. Strong protection on large areas can leave the result above the target.
  • Broken geometry. Duplicate or zero-area faces block collapses. Clean Mesh runs by itself on meshes with many duplicate vertices or zero-area triangles. Otherwise, with no preserve options on, the report says the mesh may be broken and suggests Clean Mesh. If Clean Mesh is already on, it says the mesh cannot be reduced further without breaking its shape.

The report only raises this warning when the result is clearly above the target (more than 20% over), so small differences do not show up as warnings.

Up to 2.7.0 the Preserve options (Borders, Materials, UV Seams, Sharp Edges) could stop a crush too, because a kept edge was a hard lock. Since 2.7.1 a kept edge is a cost instead, and the crush reaches the count you asked for. What a very low target costs on such a model is shape, not triangles: see What a very low target costs.

Small pieces at heavy reductions

A model made of many separate pieces (a pole with its insulators, a set of screws) loses whole pieces when the target is a small fraction of its triangles. Since 2.7.1 every connected piece keeps a minimum number of triangles until the error budget is truly spent, and then pieces go in order of how little they matter, smallest first. At an extreme target pieces still disappear; raise the target, or put a Protect Area on the ones that must stay. Remove Small Pieces under Clean Mesh is a different thing: it deletes loose bits before the crush, on purpose.

Objects can be skipped

An object is left untouched, and named in the report, when it:

  • has no geometry after modifiers,
  • has fewer than 4 triangles, or
  • gets no triangles in a Budget crush.

If every selected object is already at or below the target, the copies are left unchanged and the report says so.

Scans and noisy meshes: Sharp Edges

On a scan-like mesh, almost every edge has a small bend because of surface noise. Treating all of them as sharp would lock the whole mesh.

So on meshes that read as scans (mostly noise, few flat faces), angle-based Sharp detection is skipped. Only the edges you marked Sharp in Blender are kept. The report names the objects where this happened.

If you want specific edges kept on a scan, mark them Sharp yourself first.

Very low targets on dense CAD

Very low targets on dense CAD meshes, a few hundred triangles, can fold the walls of holes together. This leaves edges that are shared by three or more faces (non-manifold edges).

The report warns when a crushed copy has more of these edges than its source. If that matters for your export or for baking, raise the target.

Baking

  • Base Color is baked from the image textures feeding each material's Base Color, or from vertex colors when a material has none. Colors made by procedural nodes (Noise, Gradient, a plain color value) are not baked. An object with neither textures nor vertex colors is skipped for Base Color, and the report says so.
  • A bake needs UVs on the crushed mesh. Meshes without UVs are unwrapped automatically. See Bake Maps and Auto UVs.
  • The baked maps are connected in copies of the materials. If an input was already connected there, the old link is replaced.
  • The bake happens after the crush. A failed bake is listed in the report as a note and the crushed mesh is kept.

Rigs, shape keys and vertex colors

Skin weights, the Armature modifier, shape keys and vertex colors carry over to the crushed copy, with a few limits: shape keys are dropped when a modifier changes the vertex count, and vertex colors are skipped on sources over 3 million triangles. See Rigs, Shape Keys and Vertex Colors.

Keep UVs

Keep UVs is off by default (the Scan, Sculpt and Hard Surface modes turn it on when the mesh has UVs). Turn it on only when the crushed mesh must keep its UV layout. UV transfer quality depends on how much the topology changed. Moderate reductions give the cleanest results. Very heavy reductions can stretch UVs near seams. Turn on UV Seams to hold the seam lines in place.

Low Distortion refuses collapses that would fold or flatten a face's texture mapping. Up to 2.7.0 that could leave the result about 12% above the target on a mesh with many seams. Since 2.7.1 the crush relaxes the UV rules in stages once it runs out of safe collapses, so the target is reached; the last few percent of a very low target can then show some stretch near seams.

Revert

Revert needs the original. It only works when the crush was made with Keep Original on, or with Make LODs. See Keep Original and Revert.

Import and Crush

  • Reads .obj, .ply (ascii and binary) and .stl (ascii and binary). Other formats (glTF, FBX, USD) have to go through Blender's own importer.
  • Normals in the file are ignored; the result is shaded smooth. Keep Normals, Protect Area, Bake and LODs do not apply to an imported file.
  • A texture is only connected when the image is next to the model and Keep UVs is on. Textures other than the base color (map_Kd) are not read.
  • Faces with more than four corners are split as fans. A concave n-gon can come out wrong; scans do not have those.
  • Memory: the engine needs about 1 GB of RAM per 3 million triangles in the file. A 32 million triangle OBJ (2.5 GB on disk) peaked at 10 GB and crushed to 200,000 triangles in 72 seconds. On a 16 GB machine, stay under about 40 million triangles.

Platform

  • Blender 4.2 or later. 4.2 is the lowest version the extension accepts and has not been tested; the add-on is tested on Blender 5.2.
  • Windows x64, Linux x64, Linux ARM64 and macOS Apple Silicon have bundled native binaries. Intel Macs have no bundled binary.
  • The add-on needs its native binary. There is no Python fallback. If the binary is missing, the panel shows "Native binary not found" and Crush does not run.
