Benchmarks: against Blender Decimate and meshoptimizer

Where it stands

Two comparisons have been measured in full, both with the repo's own test harnesses, plus a first look at MeshLab on two small meshes. Nothing else has been, so this page makes no claims about Simplygon, InstaLOD, ZBrush Decimation Master or any other reducer.

Against Blender Decimate (2026-10-10, engine 2.3.2):

  • 2.2–3.0× faster on meshes of 1–7.5 million triangles, about even at 100k, slower (0.6×) on a scene of 103 small parts.
  • The same shape at the same triangle count. Decimate also reaches its target, so exact counts are not what sets the two apart.
  • Ahead on CAD with Hard Surface on (creases stay where they are), and on everything Decimate does not do: Keep Normals, UV-aware collapses, scan repair, Import and Crush, bakes, LOD chains, rigs and shape keys carried over.
  • Behind with Keep UVs on a part cut into many small UV islands at a very low target, because seam vertices stay on their islands.

Against meshoptimizer (2026-10-13, engine 2.7.1):

  • The same surface error, within measurement noise, on scans, sculpts and smooth CAD at keep 10% and keep 2%; slightly lower on a small CAD block.
  • On a hard-surface model of many parts the picture is mixed. Both tools' default settings stop short of the target on it; 2.7.1 fixed that for Polygon Crusher, and meshoptimizer's attribute setting still has the lower error at keep 10%. At keep 2% they are close.
  • meshoptimizer is about three times faster, on one core; Polygon Crusher uses all cores. On a single core the gap would be wider.

Against MeshLab (early readings, two meshes, see below): about the same at keep 10%; MeshLab's plain decimation is better on the small CAD block at keep 2%, and its UV-keeping variant is much worse there.

In short: a match for the best open-source simplifier on surface error, slower per core, better integrated for Blender work, and since 2.7.1 no longer the tool that falls over on multi-part hard-surface models, without being the best one on those either. The sections below show the numbers.

Against Blender Decimate

Measured on 2026-10-10 with Polygon Crusher 2.3.2. This section shows how the speed figure on the store page was measured, so you can judge it yourself.

These numbers still apply to 2.4.x. 2.4 changed the panel, the bake, and a Keep Normals correction that only runs on CAD and hard-surface parts. 2.4.2 added two small engine checks (no stray spike vertices, better detail in protected areas) that leave scan, sculpt and CAD results unchanged. 2.5.0 changed only the bake (base color from image textures, fewer tiny UV islands on scans); the crush itself is the same. 2.6.0 added Low Distortion under Keep UVs, which costs a few percent of crush time. 2.7.1 changed how kept edges and small pieces are handled; scans, sculpts and smooth CAD parts measure the same as before, hard-surface models with many parts do not (see Against meshoptimizer below). None of these were re-timed against Decimate.

Summary

  • Speed: 2.2–3.0× faster than Blender's Decimate on meshes of 1–7.5 million triangles. About the same at 100k. Slower on many small parts.
  • Shape: about the same as Decimate at the same triangle count. On CAD parts with Hard Surface on, creases stay exactly in place. With Keep UVs on, very low targets on parts cut into many small UV islands keep less shape, because seam vertices stay on their islands.
  • Imported scans: meshes imported from glTF or FBX are often split into separate pieces along their UV seams. Turn on Clean Mesh for these, or open edges can shrink and leave gaps.

Method

  • Blender 5.2.2 LTS, run headless. Windows 11, Intel i7-12700H (20 threads), 64 GB RAM.
  • Test meshes: a CC0 Poly Haven rock scan (1.26M triangles), a smaller boulder scan (98k), a 1.0M-triangle synthetic sculpt, a 7.56M-triangle subdivided scan, a CAD part (5.1k), and a set of 103 hard-surface parts (200k total).
  • Blender: Decimate modifier in Collapse mode, ratio = target ÷ input, applied to a new mesh.
  • Polygon Crusher: the add-on's own pipeline (read the mesh with UVs and normals, run the native engine with an exact target, build the result with UVs and custom normals). No bake.
  • Time: end to end, median of 3 runs.
  • Shape loss: distance from the original surface to the result, as % of the bounding-box diagonal (mean / max).

Limits of this test

  • One machine, Windows only. Your times will differ.
  • The 7.56M mesh is a subdivided scan, not a raw capture.
  • Shape numbers are measurements, not renders. They don't capture everything you see in shading.
  • Some add-on steps (scan-noise detection for Sharp Edges, crease normal checks, the report) and the UI/undo overhead are not included, for either tool.

Against meshoptimizer

Measured on 2026-10-13, engine 2.7.0 and then 2.7.1, against meshoptimizer v1.3 (the open-source simplifier behind Godot and the glTF tools), built from source. One Windows machine. This is the test that found the two 2.7.1 fixes, so it is shown here with both results.

Method

  • The engine binary alone, not the add-on, on OBJ files: the 1.26M rock scan and the 98k boulder scan from above, a 60k noisy scan, the 1.0M sculpt, a 5k sculpt, the 5.1k CAD block, an 83k smooth CAD part, and an electricity pole of 103 parts (13.4k triangles).
  • Targets: keep 10% and keep 2% of the triangles.
  • Polygon Crusher runs with the flags its mode would use (Hard Surface for the CAD parts and the pole, Scan and Sculpt for the rest). meshoptimizer runs with its defaults and, where it does better, with its attribute settings (normals and UVs, permissive).
  • Error: distance between the original surface and the result, as % of the bounding-box diagonal, 200,000 sample points each way. "Mean" is the average; "worst" is the 99th percentile, so a few stray points do not set it.
  • Time: the engine only. meshoptimizer runs on one core; Polygon Crusher uses all of them.

Scans, sculpts and smooth CAD: the same error

At keep 10%, mean error of the two tools, Polygon Crusher / meshoptimizer:

Case Mean error (PC / mo) Worst (PC / mo)
Rock scan 1.26M → 126k 0.069 / 0.069 0.16 / 0.16
Boulder scan 98k → 9.8k 0.123 / 0.127 0.28 / 0.28
Noisy scan 60k → 6k 0.173 / 0.182 0.58 / 0.48
Sculpt 1.0M → 101k 0.101 / 0.101 0.24 / 0.24
Smooth CAD part 83k → 8.3k 0.163 / 0.163 0.40 / 0.39
CAD block 5.1k → 512 0.163 / 0.188 0.38 / 0.52

Keep 2% looks the same: the two tools are within measurement noise of each other on every one of these, before and after 2.7.1. (The 1.26M scan and the 1.0M sculpt were only run with the 2.7.0 engine; 2.7.1 did not change the scan or sculpt path.)

The multi-part pole: what 2.7.1 fixed

The pole has borders, material edges, seams and sharp edges everywhere, and 103 separate pieces. It is where the old engine failed.

Pole 13.4k, keep 2% Triangles Mean error Worst error
PC 2.7.0, plain crush 268 2.48 20.7
PC 2.7.0, Hard Surface mode 7,081 (stalled at 53%) 0.21 3.3
PC 2.7.1, plain crush 266 0.75 5.2
PC 2.7.1, Hard Surface mode 266 0.42 4.2
meshoptimizer, defaults 2,788 (stopped at 21%) 0.27 5.9
meshoptimizer, attributes 260 0.39 5.7
  • At keep 2% the 2.7.0 plain crush disintegrated: whole beams went while small insulators stayed, hence a worst-case error of 20.7% of the model's size. In 2.7.1 every piece keeps a minimum until the budget is spent, and the worst case is 5.2%.
  • Hard Surface mode on 2.7.0 stalled at about half the triangles at both targets (7,091 at keep 10%, 7,081 at keep 2%), because the kept edges were a hard lock. In 2.7.1 it reaches 1,337 and 266.
  • meshoptimizer with its defaults also stops short on this model, at 2,816 triangles whatever you ask; with its attribute settings it reaches the count. At keep 10% that setting has the lowest error of all the runs (mean 0.17, worst 0.84, against 0.23 and 1.30 for Hard Surface mode). At keep 2% the two are close: lower mean for meshoptimizer, lower worst case for Polygon Crusher.

On the 5.1k CAD block, Hard Surface mode at keep 10% halved its error (mean 0.31 → 0.16, worst 1.23 → 0.38) because the hole rims are no longer locked. At keep 2% it now reaches 102 triangles where 2.7.0 stopped at 272, and that costs shape: mean error 1.98 against 1.13 at the old, higher count. Reaching a very low count on a small part is a trade you choose by picking the target.

Speed

meshoptimizer is about three times faster: 0.8 s against 2.3 s on the 1.26M scan at keep 10%, 0.5 s against 1.3 s on the 1.0M sculpt. It gets there on one core; Polygon Crusher spreads its work over all of them. Both are well under the time Blender needs to build the result mesh.

Limits of this test

  • Engine against library, both run from Python on one Windows machine. The add-on's own read, build and bake steps are not included, and neither is any glTF or game-engine pipeline around meshoptimizer.
  • meshoptimizer has more settings than the two tried here; a tuned run may do better on the pole.
  • Error is a surface-distance measurement. It does not see shading, UV stretch or whether a hole is still round; look at the result.

Against MeshLab: early readings

MeshLab's Quadric Edge Collapse Decimation (through pymeshlab 2025.7) is the reducer most people run on scans outside game studios. It was added to the bake-off harness on 2026-10-13 and has so far been run on two of the eight fixtures, on Linux, with the 2.7.1 engine. Two small meshes are not a verdict; the full set, on the Windows machine used above, is still to come. Same error measure as the meshoptimizer section; Polygon Crusher with its mode's flags, MeshLab with the filter's defaults ("plain") and with the with-texture variant that keeps the UV layout ("UVs").

Case Polygon Crusher (mean / worst) MeshLab plain MeshLab UVs
CAD block 5.1k → 512 0.163 / 0.38 0.169 / 0.41 0.50 / 4.0
CAD block 5.1k → 102 1.98 / 12.1 0.99 / 5.4 3.5 / 21.8
Sculpt 5.1k → 514 0.114 / 0.23 0.120 / 0.27 0.130 / 0.27
Sculpt 5.1k → 102 0.364 / 0.85 0.437 / 1.87 0.59 / 2.3
  • At keep 10% the two are within a few percent of each other on both meshes.
  • At keep 2% on the CAD block MeshLab's plain decimation is clearly better: half the mean error, half the worst case. Polygon Crusher's Hard Surface mode is paying there for reaching 102 triangles while still favouring the kept edges; the plain crush (no edges kept) scores 1.63 / 10.2, also behind MeshLab. This is the one case so far where another tool is ahead on a plain surface-error measure, and it will be checked on the full set. Part of the gap is the 2.7.1 decision to always reach the count: the 2.7.0 engine stopped at 272 triangles on this block with a lower error than 2.7.1 gets at 102. Keep 2% of a 5,000-triangle part is 102 triangles, which is below where either tool keeps the shape; on a part this small, pick the target by looking at the result, not the percentage.
  • MeshLab's UV-keeping variant is far worse on the CAD block at both targets: the seams constrain it the way Keep UVs constrains any simplifier, and this block has many of them.
  • On the sculpt Polygon Crusher is ahead at both targets, more so at keep 2%.
  • Times are not comparable yet: a different machine from the rest of this page, and meshes this small finish in a few hundredths of a second either way.
